How Does It Work?

We use two main techniques:

1. Downsampling (Speed Up)

Remove some audio samples to make it play faster

2. Upsampling (Slow Down)

Add more audio samples to make it play slower

Think of it like frames in a video:

More frames = slower motion

Fewer frames = faster motion

Speed Up (Downsampling)

To make audio play 2x faster:

  • Step 1: Apply a filter to prevent distortion
  • Step 2: Keep every 2nd sample, throw away the rest
  • Result: Half the samples = plays twice as fast

Example:

Original: 10,000 samples

After 2x speedup: 5,000 samples

Plays in half the time!

Slow Down (Upsampling)

To make audio play 2x slower:

  • Step 1: Insert zeros between each sample
  • Step 2: Apply a smoothing filter to fill in the gaps
  • Result: Double the samples = plays twice as slow

Example:

Original: 10,000 samples

After 2x slowdown: 20,000 samples

Plays in double the time!

Why Do We Need Filters?

Filters prevent audio from sounding broken or distorted:

For Speed Up:

Anti-aliasing filter removes high frequencies that would cause weird sounds

For Slow Down:

Interpolation filter smoothly fills in the gaps between samples

Without filter: Choppy, robotic sound

With filter: Smooth, natural sound

Technical Concepts

Sample Rate

Number of audio samples per second (usually 44,100 Hz)

Butterworth Filter

A smooth filter that doesn't distort the audio

Normalization

Adjusts volume so audio doesn't clip or distort

All of this happens automatically - you just choose the speed!
